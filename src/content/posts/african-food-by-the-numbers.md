A lot of what the world eats begins in Africa: the chocolate in your cupboard, the coffee in your cup and staples that feed hundreds of millions of people. Here's a tour in numbers. Some of them are quizzes, so guess before you read on.

## How much of the world's supply is African?

```chart
  - { label: Cassava, value: 64, note: "About two-thirds of the world's cassava (FAO data, 2020–21)" }

caption: "Years differ slightly by crop because each comes from the latest source available. See the sources at the end."
```

Yams are the standout. Africa grows almost all of them, and one country grows most of those. How much do you think?

```chart
type: meter
title: Quick quiz
question: What share of the world's yams does Nigeria grow?
quiz: true
labels: ["0%", "100%"]
mine: 69
why: "About 69%: 61 million of the world's 88 million tonnes in 2022, according to FAOSTAT. That's more than twice every other country combined."
```

## Where chocolate begins

Cocoa is grown in the tropics on three continents, but the harvest is concentrated in a small stretch of West Africa. Côte d'Ivoire alone grows more than the Americas, Asia and Oceania put together.

```chart
type: donut
title: Where the world's cocoa beans came from, 2022/23
unit: kt
center: "3 in 4"
data:
  Côte d'Ivoire: 2180
  Ghana: 680
  Cameroon: 290
  Nigeria: 280
  Rest of Africa: 204
  The Americas: 1050
  Asia and Oceania: 267
caption: "Thousand tonnes. Source: ICCO Quarterly Bulletin of Cocoa Statistics (2022/23 estimates). “3 in 4” is Africa's share. Regional totals are rounded from ICCO's percentages."
```

## The grain behind injera

Teff is a tiny grain, each seed about the size of a poppy seed, and it's the main ingredient in injera, the spongy flatbread at the centre of Ethiopian and Eritrean meals. In Ethiopia it's grown on more land than any other grain.

```chart
type: bar
title: What Ethiopia's grain fields grow
unit: "%"
highlight: Teff

caption: "Share of grain area, 2017. Source: Ethiopia's Central Statistics Agency, via a 2021 review in Agriculture & Food Security."
```

## Coffee's home country

Ethiopia is where arabica coffee comes from, and it's still Africa's largest coffee producer. Many big coffee producers export most of their crop. Ethiopia keeps a remarkable share. Guess how much stays at home.

```chart
type: meter
title: Quick quiz
question: How much of Ethiopia's coffee do Ethiopians drink themselves?
quiz: true
labels: ["None of it", "All of it"]
mine: 50
why: "About half. Estimates range from 50% to 60%, a rare thing among big coffee producers. Coffee ceremonies are a daily ritual in many Ethiopian homes."
```
